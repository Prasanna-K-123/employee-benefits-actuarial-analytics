# Excel workbook formula map

The primary spreadsheet work product is `Employee_Benefits_Actuarial_Model.xlsx`. The workbook uses visible hard-coded assumptions and formula-driven outputs. This public formula map keeps the workbook logic inspectable alongside the independent Python layer.

## Assumptions

Illustrative hard-coded inputs:

- retirement discount rate: 5%
- salary growth: 4%
- accrual rate: 1.5%
- retirement age: 65
- annuity discount rate: 4%
- annuity term: 15 years
- health PAD / risk margin: 5%
- annual health claim trend: 7%

All employee and claims data are synthetic.

## Retirement sheet

For employee row `r`:

- years to retirement: `MAX(retirement_age - age, 0)`
- projected final salary: `salary * (1 + salary_growth) ^ years_to_retirement`
- accrued annual benefit: `accrual_rate * projected_final_salary * service_years`
- annuity factor: `(1 - (1 + annuity_rate)^(-annuity_years)) / annuity_rate`
- PV at retirement: `accrued_annual_benefit * annuity_factor`
- liability proxy: `PV_at_retirement / (1 + discount_rate) ^ years_to_retirement`

The workbook sums employee-level liability proxies in Pension!I185. Change the retirement assumptions to calculate an individual scenario. The simultaneous discount-rate and salary-growth comparisons are generated by Python in outputs/pension_sensitivity.csv, not as an Excel sensitivity table. The annuity rate is separate from the pre-retirement discount rate and remains fixed in those scenarios. A zero annuity rate uses the annuity term directly.

## Health claims sheet

For each development age `j`, the age-to-age factor is:

`sum(next-development cumulative paid claims) / sum(current-development cumulative paid claims)`

Cumulative development factors are built backward from the final observed age. For each accident month:

- estimated ultimate claims: `latest_paid * cumulative_development_factor`
- IBNR: `estimated_ultimate - latest_paid`
- PAD / risk margin: `IBNR * PAD_rate`
- reserve-with-PAD proxy: `IBNR + PAD`

Claim-trend sensitivity applies 5%, 7% and 9% annual trends to estimated ultimate claims to produce illustrative next-year cost proxies.
The selected annual trend in Assumptions!D5 feeds Health Claims!P29; the three comparison rows retain their stated fixed scenario rates.

## Control philosophy

- hard-coded inputs are visually separated from formula cells;
- retirement and health modules state their scope limitations directly in the workbook;
- the independent Python layer recomputes core outputs;
- automated tests verify directional and non-negativity controls.

This is educational synthetic-data work, not a professional pension valuation, booked reserve, PwC/client work or evidence of actuarial industry tenure.
